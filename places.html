Sacred Sites

Where the Dharma was lived.

Twenty places across India and Nepal carry the living memory of the Buddha's life and the great age of Buddhist civilisation. Here are the chapters we walk with our pilgrims.

Site of Awakening

Bodhgaya, Bihar

Beneath the descendant of the original Bodhi tree, Siddhartha Gautama became the Buddha. The Mahabodhi Temple complex — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — is the spiritual heart of every pilgrimage we lead. Pilgrims circumambulate the great spire at dawn, sit beneath the tree, and join the international sangha.

● Bihar, India ● UNESCO Heritage ● 2–3 days recommended
First Sermon

Sarnath, Uttar Pradesh

In the deer park at Sarnath, the Buddha gave his first teaching to five companions and set in motion the wheel of Dharma. The Dhamek Stupa rises here still — solid, brick-built, unmoved by 1,500 years. Easily paired with Varanasi for a perfect first journey.

● Near Varanasi ● Dhamek Stupa ● 1 full day
Birthplace

Lumbini, Nepal

Across the border in the Nepalese Terai, the Mayadevi Temple marks the exact spot of the Buddha's birth. The Ashoka pillar still stands. The international monastic zone gathers temples from a dozen Buddhist nations — a quiet, leafy circuit of devotion.

● Nepal Terai ● UNESCO Heritage ● 2 days recommended
Mahaparinirvana

Kushinagar, Uttar Pradesh

In a small grove between two sal trees, the Buddha entered final nirvana. The Mahaparinirvana Temple holds a 6m reclining Buddha, gilded and serene under a single yellow cloth. Outside, the Ramabhar Stupa marks the cremation site. A quiet, profoundly affecting place.

● Uttar Pradesh ● Reclining Buddha ● 1–2 days
Great University

Rajgir & Nalanda, Bihar

For a thousand years Nalanda was the largest residential university in the world — 10,000 monks, philosophers from China to Persia. Xuanzang studied here. Nearby, the Vulture Peak above Rajgir was where the Buddha taught the Heart Sutra. Walk both in a single rich day.

● Bihar ● UNESCO Heritage ● 1–2 days
Eternal City

Varanasi (Kashi)

Older than recorded history. The ghats step down to the Ganga. Dawn boats, the evening Ganga aarti, narrow lanes, and silk weavers. We pair Varanasi with Sarnath as the natural gateway to the eastern circuit — beautiful, intense, unforgettable.

● Uttar Pradesh ● Ganga ghats ● 2 days
Last Sermon

Vaishali, Bihar

The Buddha announced his coming parinirvana here, and the second Buddhist council was held in this republic city. The Ashoka pillar stands intact — its single lion still watching over the ruins. Often combined with Kesariya's great stupa nearby.

● Bihar ● Ashoka pillar ● 1 day
Himalayan Foothills

Rishikesh & Haridwar

Where the Ganga emerges from the Himalayas. Yoga ashrams, evening aarti at Triveni Ghat, and the gateway north to Buddhist sites in Ladakh and Tibet. A beautiful add-on to any pilgrimage that wants mountain air.

● Uttarakhand ● Yoga & meditation ● 2–3 days
Cultural Add-on

Agra — Taj Mahal

Not a Buddhist site, but on the way for many international pilgrims. The Taj Mahal at sunrise is a meditation in itself. We add it for groups flying in via Delhi who want a full day before heading east to Sarnath and Bodhgaya.

● Uttar Pradesh ● UNESCO ● 1 day
Cultural Add-on

Jaipur — The Pink City

Amber Fort, the City Palace, the stepwells of Abhaneri. For pilgrims who want to extend into Rajasthan, this is our preferred 3-day add-on — small forts, slow markets, and warm desert mornings.

● Rajasthan ● Forts & palaces ● 3 days
