Bodhgaya — Mahabodhi Temple

8 sacred sites within a square mile · 3–5 days

The single most important place in Buddhism. Beneath a peepal tree on the eastern bank of the Niranjana river, Siddhartha Gautama sat for forty-nine days and woke. The Mahabodhi Temple — first built by Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, rebuilt in stone in the Gupta period — still stands over the Vajrasana, the diamond throne. Around it: the Animesh Lochana stupa where the Buddha gazed unblinking at the bodhi tree in gratitude, the Ratnachakrama meditation walk, and the great seated Buddha statue gifted by Daijokyo of Japan. We base every Indian circuit here.

Sarnath · UP
Eight great places · UNESCO

Sarnath — Dhamek Stupa

10 km from Varanasi · 1–2 days

Seven weeks after his awakening, the Buddha walked to the deer park at Isipatana and gave his first teaching to the five ascetics — the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, the turning of the wheel of dharma. The Dhamek Stupa, 43 metres of pale brick rising from a flat plain, marks the spot. Around it: the Mulagandhakuti vihara, the Ashoka Pillar (whose lion capital is now India's national emblem), and a museum holding the Sarnath Buddha — arguably the finest Buddha statue ever carved.

The garden of his birth.

Lumbini · Nepal
Eight great places · UNESCO

Maya Devi Temple, Lumbini

2 days · cross at Sonauli border

In a peaceful walled garden in the Terai lowlands of Nepal, beside a sacred pond and an ancient sal tree, Queen Maya is said to have given birth to Siddhartha Gautama in the year 563 BCE. The Maya Devi Temple shelters the marker stone identifying the exact spot — verified in 1896 by the Ashokan pillar standing nearby, whose inscription reads simply: "Here the Buddha was born." The Lumbini Development Zone, designed by Kenzō Tange, hosts monasteries from every Buddhist tradition.
