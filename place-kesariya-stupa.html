Kesariya · Bihar, India

The Relic Stupa at Kesariya

A vast brick mound rising in terraces from the Bihar plain — among the tallest Buddhist stupas on earth, and still emerging from the ground.

Region
Kesariya, Bihar
Height
~34 metres
Time needed
Half day
Best season
Nov–Feb

A short drive from Vaishali, the Kesariya stupa rises in great circular terraces from the surrounding fields — some thirty-four metres tall even in its ruined, partly-excavated state, and by some measures the tallest Buddhist stupa in the world.

The parting gift

Tradition connects Kesariya to the Buddha's departure from Vaishali. As he left for the last time, the Licchavis followed him, unwilling to let him go; to console them he is said to have given them his alms bowl. The original stupa is credited to them, later enlarged under the Guptas and the Pala kings. What stands today is a many-terraced mandala of brick, its niches once holding Buddha images, only fully recognised for its scale when excavation began in the 1990s.

Still emerging

Because so much remains unexcavated, Kesariya has an air of discovery that the more famous sites have lost. Monks circumambulate the lower terraces; the upper ones are still bound in root and earth. It is a place to feel the depth of time in this landscape — how much of the Buddhist past still lies just beneath the fields of Bihar.
