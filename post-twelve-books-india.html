Twelve books to read before walking the India circuit

The list we send every pilgrim eight weeks before departure. Not homework — provisions. Four sutras, three histories, two memoirs, three contemporary commentaries.

Written by
Ven. Tenzin Norbu
Role
Lead guide · India circuit
Published
7 February 2026
TN
Ven. Tenzin Norbu
Lead guide · India circuit · 7 February 2026

Eight weeks before departure, every pilgrim on the India circuit receives this list. I always say the same thing about it: it is not homework, it is provisions. You will walk the same ground whether or not you read these — but the ground speaks more clearly to those who arrive already listening. Read three of the twelve and you will be glad; read all twelve and Bodhgaya will feel less like arrival than return.

Four sutras

1. The Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta — the first teaching, given at Sarnath. Short. Read it three times: it is the seed of everything else, and we will read it together in the deer park.

2. The Mahaparinibbana Sutta — the account of the Buddha's last journey and death, ending at Kushinagar. It reads like a travelogue, because it is one; you will recognise our own route inside it.

3. The Heart Sutra — a single page, the distilled Mahayana, associated with Vulture's Peak at Rajgir. Do not try to understand it yet. Just carry it.

4. Selections from the Dhammapada — the pocket wisdom of the tradition, endlessly quotable. A verse a day for the eight weeks is a good discipline.

Three histories

5. Xuanzang, Records of the Western Regions (in selection) — our seventh-century predecessor's account of these very places. We read from it at Nalanda.

6. Charles Allen, The Buddha and the Sahibs — the gripping story of how nineteenth-century archaeologists rediscovered a Buddhism that India had largely forgotten. It explains why the sites look as they do.

7. A short life of Ashoka — because the emperor's pillars and edicts are the connective tissue of the whole circuit, from Lumbini to Sarnath.

Two memoirs

8. A pilgrim's memoir of the circuit — we rotate the specific title, but always a first-person account by someone who walked these places. It calibrates expectations better than any guidebook.

Read three and you will be glad; read all twelve and Bodhgaya will feel like a return.

9. A teacher's memoir of practice in India — usually a Western monastic who trained in the forest or the Himalaya. It shows the living tradition behind the ruins.

Three contemporary commentaries

10. A modern commentary on the Four Noble Truths — so that the framework is familiar before we sit at Sarnath.

11. A book on meditation you will actually practise from — one clear manual, not five. Our pre-trip online sessions build on it.

12. A single volume on Buddhist art and iconography — so that the mudras, the postures, the symbols at every site become a language you can read rather than a blur of statues.

That is the list. Your guide will send the exact editions with the pre-trip pack, and we will spend six weeks of online sessions moving through the essentials together. But start now, start small, and let the reading do quietly what it does: turn a tour into a homecoming.
